## 2. What Is Fair Use?

Fair use is a legal doctrine under U.S. copyright law (17 U.S. Code § 107) that permits limited use of copyrighted material without acquiring permission from the rights holder, under certain circumstances. Other countries have similar concepts, such as "fair dealing" in the UK, Canada, Australia, and other Commonwealth nations.

In the United States, fair use is determined on a case-by-case basis by evaluating four factors:

### Factor 1: Purpose and Character of the Use
- Is the use for commercial or nonprofit/educational purposes?
- Is the use transformative (adding new meaning, commentary, or expression)?
- Non-commercial, educational, and transformative uses are more likely to be considered fair.

### Factor 2: Nature of the Copyrighted Work
- Is the work factual (scientific, news) or creative (art, music, film)?
- Creative works receive stronger copyright protection than factual works.
- Published works are more likely to be subject to fair use than unpublished works.

### Factor 3: Amount and Substantiality of the Portion Used
- How much of the work was used, both quantitatively and qualitatively?
- Using a small, non-central portion of a work is more likely to be fair use.
- Using the "heart" of a work may weigh against fair use, even if the amount is small.

### Factor 4: Effect on the Potential Market
- Does the use harm the market for the original work or its derivatives?
- Uses that substitute for the original work (rather than comment on or criticize it) are less likely to be fair.

## 3. Personal Downloading and Fair Use

Downloading video content for personal, non-commercial use **may** be considered fair use in some circumstances, depending on:

- Whether you already have legal access to the content (e.g., through a subscription or public availability)
- Whether you are downloading for time-shifting (watching later) rather than distribution
- Whether the download replaces a purchase or paid viewing
- The nature of the content (user-generated vs. professionally produced)

**However:** Downloading content from streaming platforms often violates those platforms' Terms of Service, even if it could arguably be fair use. Terms of Service violations are separate from copyright infringement and may result in account termination or other consequences.

## 4. Jurisdiction Variance

Fair use is a distinctively U.S. legal doctrine. If you are outside the United States:

- **Canada:** Has a "fair dealing" doctrine covering research, private study, education, parody, satire, criticism, review, and news reporting
- **United Kingdom:** Has a "fair dealing" doctrine with specific categories (research, private study, criticism, review, news reporting)
- **European Union:** Copyright law varies by member state, with some countries having "private copy" exceptions that may permit downloading for personal use
- **Australia:** Has a "fair dealing" doctrine similar to the UK
- **Other countries:** Copyright laws vary widely; you should research the laws in your specific jurisdiction

## 5. User Responsibility

You should only download content using the Software if:

- You own the copyright to the content
- You have obtained explicit permission from the copyright holder
- The content is in the public domain
- The content is licensed for free download or reuse (e.g., Creative Commons)
- Your download qualifies as fair use, fair dealing, or a similar exception in your jurisdiction

**When in doubt, seek permission from the copyright holder.**

## 6. Recommendations

- Download only content you have the legal right to access
- Use downloaded content for personal, non-commercial purposes only
- Do not redistribute, re-upload, or share downloaded content without permission
- Respect the terms of service of the platforms you use
- Consult a qualified attorney if you have specific legal questions
